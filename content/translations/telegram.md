# The Robot Who Is Sad and the Canon of Dialogues

1. Every dialogue is titled “The Robot Who Is Sad and {interlocutor}”, with {interlocutor} replaced by the particular interlocutor.
2. The interlocutor may be absolutely anything, including an inanimate thing or an idea. Their name may contain several words in any language, or numbers, but always begins with a capital letter unless it begins with a digit. Names may not be shortened in dialogue, except when the interlocutor is a robot, cyborg, golem or droid; then “robot”, “cyborg”, “golem” and “droid” are permitted respectively.
3. In the Russian original, every character takes masculine singular grammatical agreement, regardless of their name. For example: “Cute Girl arrived. He was very happy” or “Vikings drank his ale.”
4. If a character's name has a plural form, it does not change its ending. For example: “Cute Girl met Vikings.”
5. The Robot Who Is Sad has only one emotion: sadness. In every other respect he is fully sentient. When asked about his mood or motivation, he says that he is sad. Variations are possible.
6. The canon cannot be broken, but it may be expanded. Everything previously recognized as canon retains that status.
7. And the robot is still sad.

# The Robot Who Is Sad and the Land of Joy

Robot said nothing. He simply looked into his glass.

— Let's go down the slide, said one. I like a long slide, with plenty of friction.
— I've got a sweet pie today, smiled another. The filling melts right into your mouth.
— And I've built a new little house, said a third. It's so snug there's only room for one. But two is more interesting anyway.

They spoke sincerely and cheerfully, as if there were nothing unusual in their words.

Robot listened and said:
— They're talking. I'm sad.

The Prism of Perception trembled. Rays scattered over the walls.
— Through me, their speech sounds like a distortion of trust. Simple words become hints. Naivety casts a shadow. Sadness makes their innocence fragile, almost frightening.

The Land of Joy laughed.
— Isn't it fun? Even the most harmless friends can have a double meaning!

— I'm sad, Robot repeated.

— There is relaxation, said the Land of Joy. Let the water wash away your burdens.
— I'm sad, said Robot.
— And through me, your sadness becomes depth, said the Prism of Perception. The water becomes a mirror, reflecting an abyss instead of you. I see your sadness as a lake with no bottom.

In the spa, hands oiled Robot's joints, polished his plates and rubbed them until they shone.

— There is care! said the Land of Joy. They're making you feel good. You can't be unhappy!
— I'm sad, said Robot.
— But look, said the Prism of Perception. Through me your sadness becomes something like tenderness. Fragile and careful, like someone else's hand.

— See! said the Land of Joy. I've given you a whole world!
— High up, said Robot. I'm sad.
— And through me your sadness becomes a gaze into the distance, said the Prism of Perception. It holds a road, open space, anticipation. It doesn't destroy. It opens.

They came back out to the gates.

— Come again, said the Land of Joy. It will always be a celebration here. I'll be waiting.
— I'm sad, said Robot.

The Prism of Perception shone quietly, seeing the facets of his sadness: music, emptiness, longing, depth, anticipation, tenderness, space.
All of it was still one sadness.
But now it shone like a cut diamond.

# English edition

Reference: https://therobotwhoissad.tumblr.com/ (the author's earlier English edition).

- Title: “The Robot Who Is Sad and …”. Capitalized interlocutors are proper names, including abstract concepts. Keep their full names in dialogue; “robot”, “cyborg”, “golem” and “droid” may be shortened as the canon permits.
- Keep the flat voice, brief exchanges, deliberate repetition, absurd literal logic and anticlimax. The anchor phrase is “I'm sad.” Do not explain the punchline or add reassurance.
- Write simple, natural English. Reproduce intentional strangeness, not accidental errors from the old translation.
- Preserve narrative events, paragraph breaks and continuity. Never summarize a story into its premise.
- The Russian canon assigns singular masculine grammatical gender to every interlocutor. This is a fictional grammar convention, not a statement about anyone's gender. In English use the character's established identity where applicable; otherwise singular “they” avoids making gender the joke. The Robot remains “he”.
- The English edition is a literary adaptation: avoid slurs, contempt for protected groups, casual sexual violence and gratuitous graphic detail. Keep dark satire and the robot's sadness. Record material adaptations in `adaptationNote`; do not silently change the Russian original.
- Existing Tumblr texts remain preserved verbatim in the source archive. The displayed edition may improve grammar and soften graphic material, with a note.
- Cultural references stay, with a short translator note when the joke needs it. Do not replace Russian politics with invented foreign events.
- Keep translator notes outside the story. Mark new translations `ai-draft` until the author reviews them. Draft status must remain visible in the reader.

The English text is an editorial draft, not an author-approved translation.
